Tessalytics 1.8.8 — a slow charge is drawn slow, and a fast one learns your car.

TWO CHANGES TO THE CHARGE FORECAST

1. BELOW 10 kW, NOTHING TAPERS

A wall box is the charger's limit, not the pack's. Ten kilowatts into any Tesla
pack is under 0.15C — nothing about the chemistry is being asked for at that
current — so the rate holds, the power line is level, and the forecast is a
straight line. 1.8.7 still bent it near the top; it no longer does.

You may see the forecast finish earlier than the car's own "time remaining". That
is real, not a mistake: the car allows for the balancing it does in the last few
percent, which the measured rate cannot see yet. Where the two differ by more than
a quarter of an hour, the card now says what the car thinks, so the screen is not
quietly printing two finishing times.

2. ABOVE 10 kW, THE APP LEARNS YOUR CAR'S TAPER

A fast charge does taper, and the shape of that taper belongs to your pack — its
chemistry, its age, how your car chooses to treat it. Nothing anyone could ship
would be right for all of those.

So the app now watches. Every DC session it sees, it records charging power against
state of charge for your car, and once it has enough it uses that shape for the
forecast instead of a fitted assumption. The live reading still sets the scale — a
cold pack or a shared stall changes the height of the curve, not its shape — and
the finish is still anchored on the car's own estimate.

When a forecast is built this way the card says "From this car's own charging
history". Until then it says something else, and both are true.

WHAT TO TEST

- A wall box: the power line should be flat and the milestone times should match
  what actually happens. This is the case 1.8.7 got wrong.
- Your first few Superchargers: the forecast will use the fitted fallback, and the
  card will not claim otherwise. After three or four sessions it should switch to
  your car's own curve — tell me whether it gets better or worse when it does.
- Nothing is learned in demo mode, and nothing is learned from wall boxes, because
  a flat curve would drag a taper straight.

The learned curve is stored on the device with the rest of the vehicle's record.
It never leaves the phone.

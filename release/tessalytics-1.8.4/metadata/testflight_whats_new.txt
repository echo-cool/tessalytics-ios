Tessalytics 1.8.4 — the app works out what pack your car was built with.

WHAT TO TEST FIRST

Settings → the car's name on the home screen → Vehicle rating.

There is a new "From your VIN" section. Your VIN names the model, the factory and
the model year; a table shipped inside the app turns those into the pack the car
left the factory with — the figure every battery-health estimate divides by, and
until now the only number you had to know and type in yourself.

1. Check the model, factory and model year are right. If your server does not
   report a VIN the section is simply absent.

2. Pick your variant. The VIN does not reliably encode it, so it is a picker,
   pre-filled from TeslaMate's trim badge where that is unambiguous. Getting this
   wrong is the one way to get a wrong answer here, so it asks rather than
   guesses.

3. Tap the pack to fill the capacity field. Nothing is applied until you save,
   and a figure you have typed yourself always wins. If Tesla changed suppliers
   during your model year — a 2023 Berlin Model Y Standard, for instance — you
   will be offered both and asked to pick; a delivery invoice or the original
   range figure will say which.

Why it matters: the app otherwise derives "capacity when new" from your recorded
history, which understates any car whose logging began after the pack had already
aged. A car rated 78 kWh new reads as 74 if TeslaMate started at 15,000 miles, and
health, capacity lost and equivalent cycles all inherit the error.

The factory is the part that is easy to get wrong, and it is why this needs the
VIN rather than the model name: a 2021 Long Range AWD Model 3 is 82 kWh from
Fremont and 79 kWh from Shanghai, and both were sold in Europe.

Tessalytics 1.8.6 — what the battery will be at, and when.

WHAT TO TEST FIRST

Plug the car in. While it is charging, the hero card puts a forecast where the
seven-day battery chart usually sits: what the charge will be in an hour, what time
it reaches your limit, and a chart of both.

The chart has two axes. Charge climbs on the left; charging power falls on the
right. The solid lines are measured — what the car has actually reported since the
cable went in, marked with the time it was plugged in. The dashed lines are the
forecast. They meet at "now", and they are drawn differently on purpose: one is a
reading and one is a guess, and you should be able to tell at a glance which you
are planning around.

Below, "This charge" carries the figures, and "When it reaches" gives a clock time
for every ten percent between here and your limit — so you can decide whether to
wait for 70 or come back for 80.

Please check:

1. The hour-ahead figure against what actually happens. This is the number worth
   getting right and the one I most want a real reading on.

2. The finishing time against the car's own. They should agree — the forecast is
   anchored on the car's estimate rather than competing with it.

3. A Supercharger especially. On a wall box the rate is flat and a straight line
   is the truth. On DC the rate falls as the pack fills, and a straight line drawn
   from the current rate promises an hour that will not happen. When that is
   happening the card says "slowing" and the curve bends. Tell me if it bends too
   much or too little.

4. A charge that is nearly done, one that has just started, and one where the app
   has only seen a minute or two. The rate is measured from the car's own reported
   charge over the last several minutes, and until there is enough of a spread to
   divide by, it should decline to guess rather than show something wild.

HOW IT WORKS, BRIEFLY

Charging is not one shape, so the app does not assume one. It fits a single number
to two things the car itself reports: the rate right now, and the time the car says
it needs to reach the limit. If those agree, the rate is flat and the line is
straight. If the car says it needs longer than the current rate implies, that
difference is the taper, and the curve bends until the two agree. No charging curve
per model is baked in, because none would be right for every pack, temperature and
cabinet.

The power forecast is scaled from the power the charger reports now, in proportion
to how the rate changes — not computed from pack size, which sounds more principled
and is worse: usable capacity is not rated capacity, and reported power includes
losses the pack never sees.

ALSO

The seven-day battery chart is unchanged when the car is not plugged in; it simply
stands aside while it is, because the question has changed from "how has it been
doing" to "when can I leave".
